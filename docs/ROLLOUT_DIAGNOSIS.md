## Normalization and decoding

Saved pre/post action mean/std tensors are identical. They match exported
training statistics within float32 rounding (maximum mean difference 3.88e-8;
std difference 9.45e-9). Normalizing and decoding actual expert target chunks
recovers physical actions with maximum absolute error 1.04e-7 (mixed rad/metre
units). Independently decoding predicted gripper values as normalized * std +
mean agrees with the saved decoder within 3.73e-9 metres.

For initial observations in three held-out episodes, normalized states/actions
match raw and exported data exactly, and instruction token tensors match.
Camera tensors differ by average 0.0073–0.0163 on a 0–1 scale; the export uses
encoded video. These checks do not prove all possible image differences harmless.

## Predictions on recorded expert observations

Tested 30 actual recorded observations across three held-out episodes, each with
three noise seeds. Among 45 predictions at early frames 0, 100, 200, 300 and 321,
mean absolute first-command gripper error is 0.456 mm. None falls below the 11 mm
training minimum. At frame 321, predicted gripper ranges are:

| Episode | VLA on recorded observation | Expert command | VLA closed-loop applied |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| train_20004 | 35.06–35.22 mm | 35 mm | 1.88 mm |
| train_21010 | 33.39–33.46 mm | 35 mm | 1.92 mm |
| train_21036 | 35.27–35.38 mm | 35 mm | 2.02 mm |

This is a diagnostic subset. It does not establish accuracy on every frame or
recovery from observations generated by the model's own actions.

## Source-matched expert replay

Replayed all 1,703 applied commands of `train_20004` through ordinary simulator
steps, on the same source-hash-verified reset. Grasp and insertion succeed. Maximum
qpos and qvel differences from the source recording are both exactly zero.
This validates that reset/interface/physics path for this episode.

## Interpretation and next experiment

Subsequent controlled swaps identify gripper-position feedback as the dominant
near-failure input; the prefix-25 control still fails to grasp. See
[GRIPPER_RECOVERY_NEXT_STEP.md](GRIPPER_RECOVERY_NEXT_STEP.md) for measured results
and the concrete experiment recipe. The paragraphs below describe the earlier
diagnostic stage, before those interventions.

The evidence supports accumulating errors during closed-loop control: predictions
on expert observations look reasonable, while policy-generated observations lead
to premature empty-gripper closure and finger self-contact. Mean/std decoding and
inability of the simulator to execute the expert task are not supported as primary
causes by these checks. The precise origin of the drift remains unresolved.

Next: rerender matched expert and failed-policy states, and compare predictions
with state/image inputs swapped independently. Check the execution prefix and
rate-limit interaction before changing training. Use clearly labeled diagnostic
interventions; do not weaken collision guards to label failed behavior successful.
Training recovery data/perturbations should follow identification of the error.
